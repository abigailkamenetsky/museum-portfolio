An enormous, wide, soaring baroque picture gallery inside a European palace,
seen from a wide angle. This is a vast grand hall, not a corridor. The room is
very wide and very tall, with a great deal of open floor between the two side
walls, wide enough that many people could walk abreast. The ceiling soars high
above, double height, and the barrel vault springs from far up the walls. Tall
arched windows line both side walls at generous intervals, letting in soft
daylight with mature green trees visible outside. Between the windows hang
large ornate carved gilt picture frames. At the far end of the hall stands a
tall stained glass window in rich reds, blues and golds. The floor is dark
walnut herringbone parquet, polished to a deep sheen that reflects the windows
and the candlelight.

The hall is richly furnished with sculpture. Full-length white Carrara marble
statues of classical figures stand along the room on tall carved marble
pedestals, posed in contrapposto with deeply carved flowing drapery, finely
detailed faces, hands and folds of cloth. White marble portrait busts on turned
marble socles are placed between them. The pedestals are carved and fluted with
gilded bases and cornices. Gilded bronze candelabra and ornate wall sconces
hold real lit candles with soft warm flames. Polished brass stanchions with
dark crimson twisted velvet ropes cordon off the artwork along the walls. Down
the centre of the wide floor lies a long Persian carpet in deep reds, golds and
midnight blue with an intricate woven medallion pattern, and several low dark
wood benches upholstered in deep red velvet sit along its length. A gilded
console table with a marble top stands against one wall. Every one of these
objects is crisply modelled and sharply detailed, with clean carved edges and
realistic marble, brass, gilt, velvet and wood materials.

The walls are covered in DEEP FOREST GREEN damask wallpaper. The green must be
very dark and heavily saturated, like dark bottle glass or deep pine needles,
close to black in the shadowed corners and only slightly lighter where light
falls on it. The damask pattern is a subtle tonal green-on-green baroque
scroll, barely visible, catching a faint silk sheen rather than contrasting.
The walls must NOT be sage green, NOT mint green, NOT olive green, NOT grey
green, NOT pale green, and NOT yellow green. They are a rich, moody, near-black
forest green. Below the green runs a cream and pale grey marble wainscot with
gilded molding and carved marble panels, and a gilded chair rail separating the
marble from the green damask above.

The ceiling is a vast barrel vault in cream and antique gold, high overhead. It
is densely covered in heavily carved gilded rinceau scrollwork, acanthus
leaves, rosettes and raised plaster ornament, arranged in panels divided by
carved transverse ribs. Set into the centre of the vault, between the ribs, is
a row of large dark oil-painted baroque fresco panels in ornate carved gold
frames, each showing figures and angels among clouds in the style of Tiepolo
and Pozzo. These ceiling paintings are dark, warm and richly coloured against
the pale cream and gold of the surrounding carved ornament. A heavy carved gilt
cornice runs along the top of the walls where they meet the vault.

The lighting is warm, moody and dramatic. Candlelight from the sconces and
candelabra pools on the wide parquet floor, grazes the gold carving and rakes
across the marble statues so their carved drapery casts real shadows, while
cool daylight falls through the arched windows. There is strong chiaroscuro,
with deep shadows in the corners and high under the vault, and glowing warm
highlights on the gilding and the polished marble. The overall mood is hushed,
reverent, dark academia, like a private palace gallery at dusk. Photorealistic,
cinematic, wide angle, expansive sense of scale, high detail.

Every picture frame on the walls must be EMPTY. Inside each frame is only a
plain, flat, dark neutral canvas with no image, no painting, no portrait, no
figure and no pattern. Do not put artwork inside the frames. No text, no
lettering, no signage, no labels, no numbers, no people and no visitors.
